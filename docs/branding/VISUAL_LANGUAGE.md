## 1. Photography Direction

### Style
- **Professional, not stock-photo sterile.** Real business environments: offices, meetings, analysts at desks. No exaggerated smiling at laptops.
- **Warmth without casualness.** Natural light, real interactions, genuine focus. No staged handshakes or pointing at whiteboards.
- **Diversity as reality.** Teams reflect actual workplace diversity — not token representation. Age, background, and role diversity shown naturally.
- **Context, not isolation.** Show people in their work context: data on screens, reports on desks, collaborative discussions. Not isolated portraits.

### What We Do Not Show
- Robots, AI visualizations, neural network diagrams, glowing brains
- People looking at screens with wonder/amazement
- "Futuristic" HUD overlays, holograms, sci-fi aesthetics
- Stock photography clichés: handshakes, thumbs up, people jumping

### Subject Matter Priority
1. Business managers reviewing reports and making decisions
2. Data and analysis interfaces (our product) in use
3. Collaborative decision-making (teams discussing findings)
4. Business environments (offices, meeting rooms, factory floors)

## 2. Illustration Principles

### When to Use Illustration
- **Empty states:** When a section has no content yet, an illustration makes the empty state feel intentional.
- **Onboarding:** Guided first-time experiences benefit from visual support.
- **Concept explanation:** Abstract concepts (Business Health, Evidence) may benefit from supporting illustration.

### When Not to Use Illustration
- **Data display:** Never replace data with illustration. Charts and numbers are our content.
- **Reports and analysis output:** Professional documents do not use illustration.
- **Navigation and chrome:** UI should be text + icon, never illustrated.

### Illustration Style
- **Minimal line art.** Single-weight strokes, limited detail, no shading.
- **Monochromatic or duotone.** Slate palette only. No full-color illustrations.
- **Consistent scale and proportion.** All illustrations should feel like part of the same family.
- **No characters.** Abstract representations of concepts, not cartoon people.

## 3. Color Emotion

### What Our Colors Communicate

**Slate (Primary):**
Calm, professional, timeless, trustworthy. The dominant color of our interface. Slate says: "We are serious. We are stable. We have been here and will be here."

**Emerald (Positive):**
Growth, improvement, health, success. Used exclusively for positive indicators: improving trends, mitigated risks, strong performance.

**Amber (Attention):**
Caution, awareness, transition. Used for warnings, medium confidence, trial status. Says: "Pay attention here" without causing alarm.

**Red (Critical):**
Risk, decline, urgency. Used sparingly for high-severity risks and negative trends. When everything is red, nothing is.

**Blue (Information):**
Trust, intelligence, action. Used for interactive elements, evidence, recommendations. Blue says: "Here's something you can act on."

### Color Proportion
- 85% Slate (backgrounds, text, borders, structure)
- 5% Emerald (positive indicators)
- 3% Amber (warnings, attention)
- 3% Red (critical risks, errors)
- 4% Blue (actions, links, information)

### Color That Does Not Exist in Our Palette
- **Purple.** The "AI color." Using purple signals "we are an AI company." We are not.
- **Pink.** Consumer, playful, unserious.
- **Neon anything.** Unprofessional.
- **Gradients in product UI.** Flat color is more professional, more accessible, and more consistent.

## 4. Executive Visual Language

### Reports and Dashboards

**The McKinsey Standard:**
A professional consulting firm's output is:
- Highly structured (executive summary → findings → details → recommendations)
- Evidence-rich (every claim sourced)
- Visually restrained (color used sparingly to highlight, not decorate)
- Executive-ready (page one tells the whole story)

ExcelPilot reports should feel like they could have come from a consulting engagement — not a chat window.

### Report Aesthetics
- **White backgrounds** for report pages. Print-ready.
- **Minimal color.** Slate text, emerald/amber/red for semantic meaning only.
- **Clear section hierarchy.** Headline → subhead → body. Consistent throughout.
- **Generous whitespace.** Information density is lower than a dashboard. This is a reading experience.
- **Typography-driven.** No charts (yet). Structure and typography create the visual interest.

### Dashboard Aesthetics
- **Card-based layout** with consistent spacing and shadow.
- **Key metrics prominent.** Large numbers with clear labels.
- **Hierarchical organization.** Health score → summary → details. Most important first.
- **Subtle color coding.** Health score color, risk severity dots, confidence badges.

## 5. Data Visualization Principles

### Current State (V1)
ExcelPilot V1 does not include charts or graphs. Analysis is text-based with structured Business Objects.

### Future Direction
When charts are introduced:
- No 3D effects. Ever.
- Minimal chart chrome. Gridlines when necessary, never decorative.
- Color only for meaning, never for decoration.
- Labels > legends. Direct labeling is faster to read.
- Consistent chart types. A bar chart should look like every other bar chart.

### Numerical Display Rules
- Currency: .4M, , ,200 (never )
- Percentages: 14.2%, -7.3% (always one decimal)
- Large numbers: 48,320 (comma separator)
- Trends: +14.2% (green), -7.3% (red), 0% (slate)
- Tabular numbers: Always use tabular-nums for alignment

## 6. Marketing Visual Language

### Landing Page
- **Hero:** Clean, typography-focused. Large headline, supportive subtext, clear CTA. Optional: subtle background gradient (slate-50 to white).
- **Problem/Solution sections:** Card grids with icons or simple illustrations.
- **Demo CTA:** Distinctive section with amber/blue accent to draw attention.
- **Security/Trust:** Emerald-themed section. Green = safety and trust.

### Social Media & Ads
- **Text-forward.** Headlines that communicate value, not curiosity gaps.
- **Screenshots over illustrations.** Show the product. It's our best marketing asset.
- **Professional color palette.** No bright backgrounds. No attention-grabbing tactics.
