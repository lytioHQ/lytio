# ExcelPilot — Brand Name Exploration v2.0

> **Status:** Living Document  
> **Version:** 2.0 | 2026-07-31  
> **Parent:** BRAND_CONSTITUTION.md Section 6  
> **Mandate:** Global-first brand. Equal strength in English and Chinese without literal translation.

---

## 0. Core Rule

> **Reject any candidate that cannot naturally evolve into a premium Chinese brand.**

A name that works only in English is not a global brand name. A name that requires a completely different Chinese brand is not a unified brand. We evaluate every candidate for bilingual strength — the name must carry the same brand feeling, premium positioning, and memorability in both languages without forced translation.

---

## 1. Evaluation Criteria

### English Dimensions (1–10)

| # | Dimension | What It Measures |
|---|-----------|-----------------|
| E1 | Clarity | Is the meaning immediately understood by English speakers? |
| E2 | Memorability | Is it easy to recall after one exposure? |
| E3 | Professionalism | Does it sound appropriate for a business product? |
| E4 | Uniqueness | Is it distinctive in the market? |
| E5 | Extensibility | Can it grow beyond spreadsheets to general business data? |
| E6 | Pronunciation | Is it easy to say and spell? |
| E7 | Domain availability | Is a reasonable domain obtainable? |
| E8 | Trademark viability | Is it defensible as a trademark? |

### Chinese Dimensions (1–10) — New in v2.0

| # | Dimension | What It Measures |
|---|-----------|-----------------|
| C1 | Pronunciation quality | Is the transliteration natural and pleasant in Mandarin? |
| C2 | Brand image | Does the Chinese name feel premium, trustworthy, and professional? |
| C3 | Memorability | Is the Chinese name easy to remember after one exposure? |
| C4 | Cross-language consistency | Does the Chinese name convey the same feeling as the English name? |
| C5 | Storytelling potential | Can the name inspire a meaningful brand narrative in Chinese? |
| C6 | Homophone safety | Are there any negative or embarrassing homophones in Mandarin or major dialects? |
| C7 | Character quality | Are the Chinese characters visually balanced, positive, and professional? |
| C8 | Long-term premium potential | Can this name anchor a premium brand for decades in the Chinese market? |

### Total Score

Final score = (Sum of E1–E8) + (Sum of C1–C8) = Maximum 160 points.

## 6. Name Selection Rules

1. **Premium Chinese brand mandatory.** Any candidate that cannot support a genuine premium Chinese brand name is rejected — regardless of English strength.

2. **Independent excellence.** Both the English and Chinese names must be independently strong. One cannot "carry" the other.

3. **No literal translation.** The Chinese name should capture the brand feeling, not translate the English words. "Veridon" → "唯睿达" is not a translation — it's a parallel brand expression.

4. **Three characters preferred.** Chinese brand names with three characters are the sweet spot: memorable, rhythmic, and visually balanced. Two characters is too short (feels generic). Four characters is acceptable for descriptive names but less premium.

5. **Character quality over phonetic accuracy.** A Chinese name with excellent semantic meaning and slightly imperfect phonetic alignment is better than a phonetically accurate but meaningless string of characters.
